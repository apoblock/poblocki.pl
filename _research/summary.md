# Research notes, 14 November 2026

Notes from checking the Polish and English family pages against published records, the Linia gravestone photograph, and what the family added by mouth. This folder is not part of the website. Guesses stay here. The pages keep the family account, cite a source when a sentence rests on one, and say when a point is unknown or still needs checking.

## What changed on the site

The family-history, Pobłocie, and arms pages, in both languages, were rewritten on the branch `update-family-history`. The black header was shortened, the text column set to about 78% of the window, the language links moved to the top right with the Polish and United States flags, and the theme’s “View on GitHub” button moved to the footer.

On the family pages, published dates and offices were added next to the family account, with links, where a record was actually found:

- Gustaw, Leon, and Juliusz from *Polski Słownik Biograficzny* (via Minakowska), including Gustaw at St Barbara in Kokoszkowy near Starogard, not Kokoszki in Gdańsk.
- Antoni’s dates, as repeated on the Linia stone: *1846-12-15, †1934-04-04.
- Otylia née Karszna, *1864-11-02, †1941-03-25, from that same stone, with Zygmunt *1893-02-17, †1937-04-19. The stone thanks the family for the land under the cemetery.
- Władysław on the board of the Gazeta Kaszubska company (registered 5 September 1922), not as its sole founder. The register’s “dr Juliusz Pabłocki” is on the supervisory board.
- Dr Juliusz as vice-president of the Gdynia regional court in 1939. The account says president.
- Stanisław, *1890-02-14 in Linia, died †1942-01-11 as a prisoner-of-war physician at Stalag XI C, buried at Bergen-Lohheide. The account says the concentration camp.
- Zygmunt on the 1926 county National Defense Committee, and as founding president of OSP Linia in 1930. The wartime body is the Ostdeutsche Landbewirtschaftungsgesellschaft (Ostland), remembered in the account as “Ostern Deutsche Landbewirtschaftung”.
- Zygmunt and Zofia née Woyke’s children, as told by the family: Bogusław (died 2014) and his younger brother Janusz are named. Bogusław and his wife Zofia — a different Zofia — lived in Szyszkowo. Their children, oldest to youngest: Grzegorz, Michał, Cezary, Anna. The account’s other son and the daughter are not named.
- Photographs: the Linia and Pobłocie road signs, the Linia gravestone, and the present gmina office. The office caption does not claim the standing building is the old manor.

Pobłocie: Kashubian Pòbłocé, first record 1400 or 1401, the 1772/1773 cadastre, an 11-łan estate still in 1880, parceling in the second half of the 19th century. One sentence says the Pobłocie near Kołobrzeg (German Pobloth, 1159) is a different village.

Arms: variant Ig is the one on Augustyn of Linia’s 1872 seal. At least seven variants. Pragert calls it a Brochwicz variant, Ostrowski a Jeleń variant. Tinctures of Ig are unknown.

## Władysław and Kazimiera in America

What they did in America is not known. No passenger list, census entry, or city directory found in this search names both of them, or names Kazimiera at all.

What is known about him after the return:

- The family account says he came back from America, was a sworn translator of English, French, German, Russian, and Latin, had no children with Kazimiera, and lived and died in Wejherowo.
- On 5 September 1922 he is on the board of the Gazeta Kaszubska company in Wejherowo. That is the earliest dated record of him back in Poland. It does not say when he returned, or from which American place.
- The Wejherowo directory published by Feliks Jeuthe of Grudziądz, dated by the library “about 1925,” lists “Pobłocki … Władysł., dziennikarz, Hallera 5,” and on the same line “Dr. Juljusz, sędzia, Dworcowa 2.” Kazimiera is not named. A later Piaśnica biography cites this same scan as 1922. The library date is the one used here. http://kpbc.umk.pl/Content/215668 and https://kpbc.umk.pl/dlibra/publication/216428

One American book fits the name and the years, and is not identified as this man:

- *Nowy malowniczy atlas świata. Świat po wojnie, nowa Polska, traktaty pokoju*, compiled by a Władysław Pobłocki, copyright 1920, printed and published by the Worzałł brothers in Stevens Point, Wisconsin. 384 pages, maps and photographs, written for Polish readers in America. Catalog: https://www.rcin.org.pl/dlibra/publication/11134/edition/6725/content Description of the contents: http://dawnemapy.blogspot.com/2013/02/nowy-malowniczy-atlas-swiata-1920.html
- Stevens Point was a Polish publishing town. The Worzałł (Worzalla) brothers printed *Rolnik* and *Gwiazda Polarna* there. The atlas does not say the compiler lived in Stevens Point, only that they printed it. No preface found online names a wife, a hometown, or a newspaper job.
- The dates allow it: the atlas is 1920, and this Władysław is a journalist in Wejherowo by about 1925, already on a newspaper board in 1922. That is a hypothesis. It is not on the website.

These are different people and were not used:

- Ben Poblocki of Milwaukee, who started a sign company in 1932. https://signsofthetimes.com/poblocki-celebrates-75-years/
- John Poblocki and his wife Mary née Jakubowski of Chicago, she born in Posen and died there in 1937. https://www.sbags.org/immigrant/d0461/I48740.html
- Jan Władysław Pobłocki, Pomeranian burgrave in 1695–1712. https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_Pob%C5%82ocki

To go further needs a passenger list or a Stevens Point paper that names him, and anything that names Kazimiera. Those were not in the open web sources searched.

## Needs clarification

Nothing below is stated as a fact on the site.

### Emilia’s birth, and Maria and Anna

The account writes Emilia’s birth twice: *1880-01-06 in her short biography, and *1884-01-06 in the Chełmno grave list. The death, †1907-10-30, is the same in both places. Her mother Ernestyna was born *1815-08-14, her father Augustyn *1808-08-25. Either written birth year makes Ernestyna about 65 and Augustyn about 72.

A MyHeritage check reported “Born June 1, 1880” and the same age gap. That June date is the site’s *1880-01-06 read in month-day order. It is not a third date.

The same grave list calls Maria (*1865-01-01, †1934-11-18) and Anna (*1870-05-04, †1983-06-03) Emilia’s daughters. Written this way, the daughters are born before the mother, and Anna’s lifespan is 113 years. The pages now say the dates do not make one family and that the stone has to be read. They do not offer a corrected year.

A reading discussed and not published: *1860-01-06, if a handwritten 6 was read as an 8. That would make Ernestyna about 44 and Augustyn about 51, and it would put Emilia three years after Leon (*1856). It would still not make her the mother of Maria, Anna, or of a Tadeusz born in 1872. A birth nearer Antoni’s (*1846), for example a misread 1844, would fit those daughters better, and is equally unproven. Neither guess goes on the site.

### Tadeusz Czarnowski

The account says Emilia’s son Tadeusz directed the Gdańsk railway directorate until 1939, had a private saloon car, died in Jelenia Góra, and that his wife died in Wrocław. The published Tadeusz Czarnowski was born *1872-08-22 in Łebno, died †1950-12-27 in Wrocław, directed the railways from 1920 to 30 September 1929, and married Stefania née Prabucka on 10 November 1909. The pages set those two descriptions side by side and say it is not known whether they are the same man or whether he was Emilia’s son. The possible link through the Prabuckis is only that his wife’s maiden name is Prabucka. That is not enough to identify him.

### Nowa Wieś

The account says Golub county. Władysław Prabucki’s chronicle says Jan bought the estate on 14 November 1889 in Wąbrzeźno county and transferred it on 23 March 1908. A present-day Nowawieś is in gmina Golub-Dobrzyń. The page names that village and says the identification is not confirmed.

### The Chełmno stone against the biographies

The family list gives Juliusz’s death as 8 February 1915. The biographical dictionary gives 9 February 1915. It gives Leon as a monk, born 12 September 1856, died 14 November 1900. The dictionary gives 11 September 1856 and 11 November 1900, and calls him a historian, teacher, and missionary, not a monk. The grave list on the site is the family’s transcription again. The biographies keep the dictionary dates and say where the account differs. Nobody has re-read the stone.

### Two Zofias, and the unnamed children

Zofia née Woyke, Zygmunt’s wife, and Zofia, Bogusław’s wife, are different people. Bogusław’s wife’s maiden name was not given. The account says Zygmunt and Zofia Woyke had three sons and a daughter. Only Bogusław and Janusz are named. The other son and the daughter stay unnamed.

### Not this family

Do not merge in the Kołobrzeg Pobłocie, the later priest on the Olęcki stone (1966–1993), the contractor Zygmunt Pobłocki of Sierakowice, the policeman Janusz Pobłocki of Kościerzyna, Ewa Pobłocka, or Kacper Pobłocki. Jan Pobłocki *1875-10-03 †1957-03-07 on the Linia cemetery (Grobonet) is unidentified. The church plaque that mentions Zygmunt’s donation is cited in the text. The photograph is too small to read, so it was not placed on the page.
