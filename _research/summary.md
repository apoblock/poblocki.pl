# Research notes, 14 November 2026

Notes from checking the Polish and English family pages against published records, the Linia gravestone photograph, and what the family added by mouth. This folder is not part of the website. Guesses stay here. The pages keep the family account, cite a source when a sentence rests on one, and say when a point is unknown or still needs checking.

## What changed on the site

The family-history, Pobłocie, and arms pages, in both languages, were rewritten on the branch `update-family-history`. The black header was shortened, the text column set to about 78% of the window, the language links moved to the top right with the Polish and United States flags, and the theme’s “View on GitHub” button moved to the footer.

On the family pages, published dates and offices were added next to the family account, with links, where a record was actually found:

- Joanna in the Polish Biographical Dictionary index, Ernestyna on the Chełmno stone. Both names stay.
- Gustaw, Leon, and Juliusz from *Polski Słownik Biograficzny* (via Minakowska), including Gustaw at St Barbara in Kokoszkowy near Starogard, not Kokoszki in Gdańsk.
- Antoni’s dates, as repeated on the Linia stone: *1846-12-15, †1934-04-04.
- Otylia née Karszna, *1864-11-02, †1941-03-25, from that same stone, with Zygmunt *1893-02-17, †1937-04-19. The stone thanks the family for the land under the cemetery.
- Władysław on the board of the Gazeta Kaszubska company (registered 5 September 1922), not as its sole founder. The register’s “dr Juliusz Pabłocki” is on the supervisory board.
- Dr Juliusz as vice-president of the Gdynia regional court in 1939. The account says president.
- Stanisław, *1890-02-14 in Linia, died †1942-01-11 as a prisoner-of-war physician at Stalag XI C, buried at Bergen-Lohheide. The account says the concentration camp.
- Zygmunt on the 1926 county National Defense Committee, and as founding president of OSP Linia in 1930. The wartime body is the Ostdeutsche Landbewirtschaftungsgesellschaft (Ostland), remembered in the account as “Ostern Deutsche Landbewirtschaftung”.
- Zygmunt and Zofia née Woyke’s children, as told by the family: Bogusław (died 2014) and his younger brother Janusz are named. Bogusław and his wife Zofia — a different Zofia — lived in Szyszkowo. Their children, oldest to youngest: Grzegorz, Michał, Cezary, Anna. The account’s other son and the daughter are not named.
- Photographs: the Linia and Pobłocie road signs, the Linia gravestone, and the present gmina office. The office caption does not claim the standing building is the old manor.

Pobłocie: Kashubian Pòbłocé, first record 1400 or 1401, the 1772/1773 cadastre, an 11-łan estate still in 1880, parceling in the second half of the 19th century. One sentence says the Pobłocie near Kołobrzeg (German Pobloth, 1159) is a different village.

Arms: variant Ig is the one on Augustyn of Linia’s 1872 seal. At least seven variants. Pragert calls it a Brochwicz variant, Ostrowski a Jeleń variant. Tinctures of Ig are unknown.

A 17th-century Wojciech Pobłocki, a Mirachowo juror, is a different man and was not used. The stone dates Zygmunt’s death. It does not say how or where he died.

## Władysław and Kazimiera in America

What they did in America is not known. No passenger list, census entry, or city directory found in this search names both of them, or names Kazimiera at all.

What is known about him after the return:

- The family account says he came back from America, was a sworn translator of English, French, German, Russian, and Latin, had no children with Kazimiera, and lived and died in Wejherowo.
- On 5 September 1922 he is on the board of the Gazeta Kaszubska company in Wejherowo. That is the earliest dated record of him back in Poland. It does not say when he returned, or from which American place.
- The Wejherowo directory published by Feliks Jeuthe of Grudziądz, dated by the library “about 1925,” lists “Pobłocki … Władysł., dziennikarz, Hallera 5,” and on the same line “Dr. Juljusz, sędzia, Dworcowa 2.” Kazimiera is not named. A later Piaśnica biography cites this same scan as 1922. The library date is the one used here. http://kpbc.umk.pl/Content/215668 and https://kpbc.umk.pl/dlibra/publication/216428

One American book fits the name and the years, and is not identified as this man:

- *Nowy malowniczy atlas świata. Świat po wojnie, nowa Polska, traktaty pokoju*, compiled by a Władysław Pobłocki, copyright 1920, printed and published by the Worzałł brothers in Stevens Point, Wisconsin. 384 pages, maps and photographs, written for Polish readers in America. Catalog: https://www.rcin.org.pl/dlibra/publication/11134/edition/6725/content Description of the contents: http://dawnemapy.blogspot.com/2013/02/nowy-malowniczy-atlas-swiata-1920.html
- Stevens Point was a Polish publishing town. The Worzałł (Worzalla) brothers printed *Rolnik* and *Gwiazda Polarna* there. The atlas does not say the compiler lived in Stevens Point, only that they printed it. No preface found online names a wife, a hometown, or a newspaper job.
- The dates allow it: the atlas is 1920, and this Władysław is a journalist in Wejherowo by about 1925, already on a newspaper board in 1922. That is a hypothesis. It is not on the website.

These are different people and were not used:

- Ben Poblocki of Milwaukee, who started a sign company in 1932. https://signsofthetimes.com/poblocki-celebrates-75-years/
- John Poblocki and his wife Mary née Jakubowski of Chicago, she born in Posen and died there in 1937. https://www.sbags.org/immigrant/d0461/I48740.html
- Jan Władysław Pobłocki, Pomeranian burgrave in 1695–1712. https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_Pob%C5%82ocki

To go further needs a passenger list or a Stevens Point paper that names him, and anything that names Kazimiera. Those were not in the open web sources searched.

## Needs clarification

Nothing below is stated as a fact on the site.

### Emilia’s birth, and Maria and Anna

The account writes Emilia’s birth twice: *1880-01-06 in her short biography, and *1884-01-06 in the Chełmno grave list. The death, †1907-10-30, is the same in both places. Her mother Ernestyna was born *1815-08-14, her father Augustyn *1808-08-25. Either written birth year makes Ernestyna about 65 and Augustyn about 72. That conflict stays here. The published biography does not give a birth date, and it does not say the year still has to be read on the stone. The grave list on the site is still the family’s transcription, including *1884-01-06.

A MyHeritage check reported “Born June 1, 1880” and the same age gap. That June date is the site’s *1880-01-06 read in month-day order. It is not a third date.

The same grave list calls Maria (*1865-01-01, †1934-11-18) and Anna (*1870-05-04, †1983-06-03) Emilia’s daughters. Written this way, the daughters are born before the mother, and Anna’s lifespan is 113 years. The published pages do not discuss that. They give the family transcription and do not offer a corrected year.

A reading discussed and not published: *1860-01-06, if a handwritten 6 was read as an 8. That would make Ernestyna about 44 and Augustyn about 51, and it would put Emilia three years after Leon (*1856). It would still not make her the mother of Maria, Anna, or of a Tadeusz born in 1872. A birth nearer Antoni’s (*1846), for example a misread 1844, would fit those daughters better, and is equally unproven. Neither guess goes on the site.

### Tadeusz Czarnowski

The pages say Tadeusz Czarnowski was Emilia’s son: director of the Gdańsk railway directorate until 1939, a private saloon car, died in Jelenia Góra, his wife in Wrocław. They do not compare that with the published life. That life is a Tadeusz Czarnowski born *1872-08-22 in Łebno, died †1950-12-27 in Wrocław, director from 1920 to 30 September 1929, married to Stefania née Prabucka on 10 November 1909. Whether he is the same man is not settled here. The only suggested link through the Prabuckis is his wife’s maiden name, and that is not enough to identify him. https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadeusz_Czarnowski and https://gdansk.gedanopedia.pl/gdansk/?title=CZARNOWSKI_TADEUSZ%2C_in%C5%BCynier%2C_patron_gda%C5%84skiej_ulicy

### Nowa Wieś

The page follows the family and places Nowa Wieś in Golub county. It gives the purchase of 14 November 1889 and the transfer of 23 March 1908, and it does not name a county for that purchase or point to a present-day village. Władysław Prabucki’s chronicle says the purchase was in Wąbrzeźno county. A present-day Nowawieś is in gmina Golub-Dobrzyń. Whether that is the estate is not confirmed. https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowawie%C5%9B_(wojew%C3%B3dztwo_kujawsko-pomorskie)

### The Chełmno stone against the biographies

The family list gives Juliusz’s death as 8 February 1915. The biographical dictionary gives 9 February 1915. It gives Leon as a monk, born 12 September 1856, died 14 November 1900. The dictionary gives 11 September 1856 and 11 November 1900, and calls him a historian, teacher, and missionary, not a monk. The grave list on the site is the family’s transcription. The biographies use the dictionary dates and do not mention the difference. Nobody has re-read the stone.

The Linia stone repeats Antoni *1846-12-15, †1934-04-04, and Zygmunt *1893-02-17, †1937-04-19. The page gives those dates and does not add that the stone repeats them. The Chełmno stone says Ernestyna; the biographical-dictionary index says Joanna. The page uses Joanna Ernestyna and does not compare the two. The company register spells the judge “dr Juliusz Pabłocki”. An interwar list of Pomeranian estates names Jan Woyke at Łężyce and Zygmunt Pobłocki at Linia. None of those source checks are on the page.

### Two Zofias, and the unnamed children

Zofia née Woyke, Zygmunt’s wife, and Zofia, Bogusław’s wife, are different people. Bogusław’s wife’s maiden name was not given. The account says Zygmunt and Zofia Woyke had three sons and a daughter. Only Bogusław and Janusz are named. The other son and the daughter stay unnamed.

### Not this family

Do not merge in the Kołobrzeg Pobłocie, the later priest on the Olęcki stone (1966–1993), the contractor Zygmunt Pobłocki of Sierakowice, the policeman Janusz Pobłocki of Kościerzyna, Ewa Pobłocka, or Kacper Pobłocki. Jan Pobłocki *1875-10-03 †1957-03-07 on the Linia cemetery (Grobonet) is unidentified. The church plaque that mentions Zygmunt’s donation is cited in the text. The photograph is too small to read, so it was not placed on the page.

## Münster packet, scanned 14 November 2026

Twenty-nine photographs in the family papers, mostly German. They are a letter and a booklet from Annemarie and Fritz von Poblotzki of Münster (Berg Fidel), written to the Polish side of the family, plus a 1979 Polish biographical sketch they used. Reference scans are in `_research/munster/`. Private addresses and telephone numbers in the letters stay in the original scans only.

The pages now include only what this packet states in agreement, and what does not contradict a record already on the site: Wojciech’s dates and career, including Niedźwiedź from 1896; Gustaw’s birthday and his move to Wtelno in 1905; Leon’s Resurrectionist and Lazarist years; Juliusz’s studies in Münster, the deanery from 1888, and the honorary canonry from 1904; Władysław as a philologist and surveyor, back from years of work in America, editor of *Gazeta Kaszubska* from 1928 to 1939. The volume is *Zasłużeni ludzie Pomorza Nadwiślańskiego z okresu zaboru pruskiego*, edited by Józef Borzyszkowski and others, Gdańsk 1979. The typescript signs the sketch “Józef Bożyszkowski.” Catalog record: https://repozytorium.bg.ug.edu.pl/info/book/UOG6c8d6d03405349bf9681bfdc4db1610a

Fritz’s booklet is *Drei Studenten der Theologie der Familie von Poblotzki aus Linde in Westpreußen in Münster in Westfalen im 19. Jahrhundert*, researched and presented by him in Münster in March 2003 (10 copies), reprinted unchanged in 2009 (5 copies). It copies the matriculation lists of the Königliche theologische und philosophische Akademie zu Münster. A shorter genealogy, “Eine kurze genealogische Skizze zu einem Poblotzki-Haus in Linde / Linia”, sits with it. Its notes cite letters from Dr J. Bożyszkowski of 29 February 1981 and 16 July 1983, and Fritz’s own *Die Familie von Poblotzki im Zeugnis der Literatur* (Münster 1980). The Polish sketch is in Gdańskie Towarzystwo Naukowe, *Zasłużeni ludzie Pomorza Nadwiślańskiego z okresu zaboru pruskiego. Szkice biograficzne*, series “Pomorze Gdańskie” no. 12, Gdańsk 1979, edited by Józef Borzyszkowski and others. The typescript signs it “Józef Bożyszkowski.”

What the sketch writes, and where it differs from the page:

- August, son of Adalbert de Poblocki and Constantia Philippina Jacobina de Selasinska. Teacher in Łężyce 1835–1840, then Linia. The sketch writes his birth as 25 July 1808. The page, from the Chełmno list, has 25 August 1808. Death 17 May 1886 agrees. Ernestyna’s dates agree. Her brother is called a major. The page’s Wojciech Delk, Zielezińska, and Milczewska are not in this sketch, and Selasińska is not on the page. Those parents are not identified with each other here.
- Seven children, matching the page’s five sons and two daughters. Emilie is written *6 January 1844, †30 October 1907. That year is not 1880 and not 1884. It is what this sketch writes. It is not a new reading of the stone, and it is not on the page. Matylda’s dates are left blank.
- Juliusz: birth written 28/29 November 1835 in Łężyce. School in Lębork and Chojnice to 1857. Studies in Pelplin, possibly Berlin, and Münster. Ordained in Chełmno in 1862. Habilitation 9 July 1864, *De Herodiani vita, ingenio, scriptis*. Eight years at the Collegium Marianum in Pelplin. Parish priest in Chełmno 1872–1915. Death 9 February 1915. The Münster lists put him there from the summer semester of 1859 through the winter semester of 1861–62, birthplace given as Lensitz, later “Lensitz, Prov. Preußen.” Bożyszkowski adds that Bishop Jan Nepomucen Marwitz named him parish priest in the hardest years of the Kulturkampf, dean of Chełmno in 1888, honorary canon in 1904, and wanted him as auxiliary bishop of Chełmno. The Prussian government did not agree, and Marwitz did not press it. The page gives the deanery and the canonry. It does not give the Kulturkampf story or the auxiliary bishopric. That is a different text from the bells article.
- Gustaw: *21 September 1840 in Łężyce. School in Wejherowo and Chełmno to 1866. Ordained 1870. Chaplain in Tuchola and Chełmno 1875–1886, then administrator in Koronowo and Działdowo, parish priest in Kokoszkowy from 1889. The sketch says that in 1894 the parish endowment there was 92 hectares, and that from 1905 he held the parish of Wtelno near Bydgoszcz and died there on 25 November 1915. The page now gives the birthday, Kokoszkowy, and Wtelno. It does not give the 92 hectares. Münster lists: winter 1866–67 through winter 1867–68, birthplace Linia.
- Wojciech, called Adalbert: *6 October 1853 in Linia. School to 1873. Theology in Münster and Innsbruck. Ordained 1878. Doctorate in Rome in 1881. Vicar in Chełmno with Juliusz until 1886, then administrator in Wabcz, and from 1896 parish priest in Niedźwiedź. In 1899 he founded Towarzystwo Wiecowe in Chełmno. He published the 1647 visitation of Bishop Andrzej Leszczyński. Death 17 October 1923. Fritz’s German sketch puts a question mark on Niedźwiedź and also writes Niedźwiedzica (Bärwalde) in the Danzig district, after an old topography that only says Bärwalde had a Catholic filial church. Bożyszkowski, and the family page, say Niedźwiedź. The page now gives this career. It does not mention Niedźwiedzica. Münster lists: winter 1873–74 and summer 1874, birthplace Linia. A roommate was Constantin Podlaszewski of Grzybno.
- Leon: *12 September 1856 in Linia, which is the family grave’s day, not the dictionary’s 11 September. Studies in Innsbruck and Königsberg, a dissertation on the history of Lithuania, Resurrectionist, Rome, then a mission in Adrianople. After an illness he joined the Lazarists in Kraków. Died in Chełmno, with Juliusz, on 11 November 1900. The page keeps the dictionary dates and now also gives the Resurrectionist mission and the Lazarists.
- Antoni: the sketch writes death on 23 April 1934. The page, from the Linia stone, has 4 April 1934. Otylia’s dates agree. It says the marriage produced eight children and that their descendants, over five generations, are a living branch in Poland. It does not name the eight.
- Of those eight, Bożyszkowski names four. Władysław, a philologist and surveyor, after many years of work in America, editor of *Gazeta Kaszubska* from 1928 to 1939, is now on the page, together with the 1922 board and the Wejherowo directory. His birth and death are not, because the sketch’s “(1885–1939)” is only a parenthetical and collides with Władysław Prabucki, born in 1885. Dr Juliusz as president of the Gdynia court, dead in the first year of the Nazi camps, is not on the page: the 1939 record used there calls him vice-president, and the camp death is this sketch alone. Stanisław as a reserve lieutenant murdered in 1939 is not on the page: the page keeps the 11 January 1942 death at Stalag XI C. Jadwiga tied to Gryf Pomorski and a husband from Gdynia is not on the page: the page keeps Szukalska of Lipinki.

The 1872 seal drawing is captioned Augustin/August v. Pobłocki of Linia, copied from the Geheimes Staatsarchiv Preußischer Kulturbesitz, Merseburg, Rep. 176 VI P 134. The seal has no tinctures. A letter of 28 December 2006 proposes colors because most other drawings use them: blue shield, gold stars, silver stag and crescent, steel-colored helmet and crown, blue and silver mantling, silver middle feather, blue outer feathers. A painted achievement lettered “Familie von Poblotzki aus Linde” was made later by a heraldic workshop in Bavaria. The arms page still says the tinctures of variant Ig are unknown. The proposal is not a reading of the seal.

The same letters enclose a description of Pobłocie in Kreis Kolberg-Körlin, German Pobloth, first recorded in 1159, with photographs Anne and Fritz took in July 1997 of Klein-Pobloth (Pobłocie Mł., Mühle-Poblotz) and Groß-Pobloth (Pobłocie Wlk.). That is the other village. It is not the family’s Pobłocie in gmina Linia, and those photographs were not copied in.

Scans kept for reference, under `_research/munster/`: the 28 December 2006 letter on the colors, the 1872 seal, the later painted arms, the genealogy sketch, three pages of the 1979 sketch, the Münster booklet cover, and the matriculation pages for Juliusz, Gustaw, and Wojciech.
